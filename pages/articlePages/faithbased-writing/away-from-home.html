You've been away from home for too long, it's time to return

John 15:13

Published on March 28, 2026 • By Nicolletta Eneji • 10 min read

I’ve been a Christian for the most part of my life—all my life, even. On paper, my spiritual resume was stacked. Christian by birth, by name, by passed catechism exams, and even by the church leadership titles I collected in high school. But if you stripped away the labels, I was hollow. I had no reason for being a Christian beyond the fact that I was born into a Christian home.

I knew God loved us, and I knew Christ’s death was proof of that love, because they were the right answers to Sunday school questions. But I couldn’t explain how or why that love, expressed through His death, was supposed to be the very foundation of my faith.

I knew God loved me because the Bible said so. But it didn’t make sense that His love required Him to become a man and die so that we wouldn’t "perish".

What did “perish” even mean?

I was told it meant eternal damnation—separation from God. But if I did enough good, why would I still be separated from Him? Wouldn’t that count for anything?

I had questions.

I questioned why God had to become a man and die when, in His might, He could simply speak a word and reverse the curse. After all, didn’t He create the world through spoken words?

Why was mankind cursed in the first place? What did people mean when they said, “man fell”? Where exactly did we fall to?

And when I began to understand this idea of "falling", another question came. If God knows everything, then He must have known we would fall. So why didn’t He stop it? Why allow us to fall only to come and save us? Why not prevent it entirely and save Himself and us the stress?

These questions stayed in my heart. I never asked them out loud because it felt almost diabolical to do so. But I asked God in my head, hoping He would answer audibly... even though the answers were already in the scriptures.

In 2022, I changed schools. And looking back now, I believe it was a very intentional move by God for the sake of my salvation.

The first way he revealed himself to me was as a helper, an academic helper to be precise.

I was switching to a course my father wasn’t exactly sold on, but I wanted it with everything in me. I needed to excel in this new path, if only to prove that the switch was worth it. I didn’t have a deep relationship with God back then, but I was aware of His “helper” reputation. My mom, my Sunday school teachers, and basically everyone who believed in something said He helped people. So, I took their word and reached out to him.

I didn’t know how to pray properly. It was always “thank you for this” and “thank you for that” until five minutes passed and I was back to my routine. But I knew how to write, so I started writing letters to Him—listing the exact grades I wanted in each course and imagining those letters being mailed to heaven.

After three consecutive semesters of receiving exactly what I had written for each course, I became certain that God could hear me—and that He was actually receiving my letters. That realization stirred something in me. It was a thirst.

In that school, I met people who referred to Him as their “friend”. They spoke about Him like someone they could converse with, and it sounded so strange to me because I had always known God as a distant almighty figure. One who knew us by name, provided for us, and answered prayers, but not someone who talked.

Some people even said he had a sense of humour, and I would wonder...are these people okay?

What do you mean, the Alpha and Omega, the Lord of Hosts, the I AM, the Beginning and the End... has a good sense of humour?

It didn’t make sense to me, but something had already started growing in me. A yearning. And sometimes that’s all God needs, a simple yearning.

He wants us to want Him. Not just as Lord but also as a friend. He wants to guide you the way your friend would guide you, to talk with you the way you talk with people you love. But that kind of relationship can only happen through salvation.

Ask and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. (Matthew 7:7 NKJV)

I sought Him, and I found Him.

In the summer of 2024, God finally reached out to me. But here’s the thing, he didn’t start with conversations. God doesn’t begin with "gisting". You cannot fully partake in that level of intimacy unless you are one with Him. And the only way to become one with Him is through salvation—after you’ve heard, accepted, and believed the gospel.

So here is the gospel:

First, God loves us deeply and yes, recklessly. He loves us so much that He bore the inconvenience of becoming a man, leaving His magnificent nature to come and die for our sins—sins that had already condemned us. He did this so we could regain the eternal life we lost through disobedience in the Garden of Eden. Through His death, He redeemed us and made a way for us to be one with Him again.

If you’re hearing this for the first time, or even the tenth, you might still be confused. And honestly, I don’t blame you. I heard it many times and still didn’t understand. I said I believed because I was afraid of going to hell. But how can you truly believe what you do not understand?

That’s why I’m here. To break this down as simply as possible, so you can truly understand the message of salvation and come home to the One who loves you.

Salvation

On a general note, salvation means deliverance or preservation from harm, ruin, or peril. It is synonymous with “redemption”, “rescue”, and “saving”, and stands in contrast to “downfall”, “damnation”, and “destruction”.

So what exactly are we being saved from?

Let’s go back to the beginning.

In the book of Genesis, God created man in His image and likeness—reflecting His nature. He gave man dominion over every living thing and blessed him: “Prosper, reproduce, fill the earth, and take charge” (Genesis 1:26-29 NKJV).

This shows that man was created to be godlike and to lead on earth. The only One above man is God Himself, in whose image we were created. Life, as it was designed, was meant to be perfect. Then came the instruction in Genesis 2:16-17:

And the Lord God commanded the man, “You are free to eat from any tree in the garden; 17 but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for when you eat from it, you will certainly die.” (Genesis 2:16-17 NLT).

Did they die?

Yes.

Did they die the way we expected? No.

Let me explain.

In Genesis 3:4-5, the enemy came as a serpent and told Eve, “You certainly won’t die.” He countered what God had said, implying that He had lied. And then added something more deceptive—that they would become like God.

But here’s the irony; God had already created man in his image and likeness. We were already his reflection. Anything beyond that would mean becoming omnipotent, omniscient, and omnipresent, and that was never meant for man.

Even though we were made like Him, we are not Him.

And we were already created in the highest authority that we could occupy. Higher than principalities, higher than powers, higher than the devil himself, and every other living thing on earth. Yet, Satan, who we were already above by virtue of our likeness to God, was advertising “godlikeness”.

You see how deceptive he is? If he knew how to be like God, why didn’t he do the abracadabra for himself? Why advertise it to someone else when he himself failed woefully and was cast out of heaven?

Anyway, let’s continue.

Eve ate the fruit and gave it to Adam; then something broke.

God said they would “certainly” die, and when God speaks like that, it is law. It is final.

They died, but not physically. They died spiritually.

They were separated from God. That was the death He spoke about.

In Eden, God made full provision for man. But disobedience brought a curse. It led to their expulsion from Eden’s paradise, and through that one act, all mankind came under the same condition.

We became separated from God.

Did God know that man would fall, and if He did, why did He let it happen?

One of the core descriptions of God is that He is omniscient—all-knowing. This means he knows everything: the past, the present, and the future. God told Jeremiah that he knew him before he was formed in his mother’s womb (Jeremiah 1:5), so how could he not have known that man would disobey Him?

Well, he knew.

1 Peter 1:20 tells us that Jesus’ death was part of God’s plan from the very beginning. It says Jesus was chosen and destined to be the sacrifice before the foundation of the earth was laid. In other words, before Genesis described the earth as formless and empty, before the command “let there be light”, Jesus had already been chosen to be our redeemer when we eventually fall.

This means God already foresaw the disobedience and the separation that would result from it. (See also Acts 2:23).

So if God knew that man would fall, why did He allow it? And why did He punish man so severely for something He could have prevented?

We often describe God as merciful, loving, and kind. That is why this question feels so heavy. I asked it too at some point. How could a loving Father expel His children from paradise and leave them with a curse? Doesn’t that contradict His nature?

But here is what I came to understand from the Word.

Those are not God’s only attributes.

God is also Just.

Throughout both the Old and New Testaments, He is described as a God of justice. Even in our prayers and songs, we call Him a “just” God.

To be just means to be completely righteous, fair, uncompromising, and full of integrity. A just judge in a Nigerian court will deliver judgement according to the law, regardless of whether the person standing before him is a stranger, a friend, or even family.

By human standards, true justice is rare. Emotion often interferes with judgement. Your biological father, if he were a judge, would struggle to sentence you to life imprisonment even if that were the legal penalty for your crime.

But God is not man. He is perfectly just.

And according to His law, the wages of sin is death (Romans 6:23). This means no matter who commits the sin, the consequence must follow. If God ignored the penalty, He would be merciful, but He would no longer be Just, and God cannot contradict Himself; He cannot choose mercy at the expense of justice; if His quality of being just is questioned, then His holiness is, too.

His being Just means that if you appear in court for damaging someone’s property, He will make sure you pay the fine to the last kobo. But guess what? He is the same one who provides the money for you to pay it.

Now, back to the fall of man.

God does not choose for us. From the beginning, He gave man free will—the freedom to choose (Deuteronomy 30:19). He presents the options and guides us toward the right one, but ultimately leaves the decision in our hands. In the Garden of Eden, He gave Adam and Eve access to every tree except one. He warned them of the consequences, but didn’t force their hand. When the serpent came and they chose disobedience, the consequence had to follow because God is Just.

Mercy and Justice?

Now remember, God is not only just; He is also merciful. A perfect blend of a loving father and an incorruptible judge.

Mercy means that there must be a second chance; it means forgiveness for the sin that caused our separation. God could not abandon us; so, while justice required that the law of sin be fulfilled, mercy made room for restoration. In fact, I believe the first account of mercy happened in the same garden where the fall took place.

And the Lord God said, “Look, the human beings have become like us, knowing both good and evil. What if they reach out, take fruit from the tree of life, and eat it? Then they will live forever!” (Genesis 3: 22 NLT).

Let me explain how this shows God’s love.

Before the fall, man was free to eat from any tree except one. So why was the Tree of Life suddenly off-limits?

The tree of life is a symbol of immortality, perpetual and eternal life. Eating from it after the fall would have meant living forever in a broken and cursed state. That was never God’s design.

While His plan had always been for us to live eternally, receiving immortality in a fallen state would have been a perpetual nightmare. By sending Adam and Eve out of Eden, God wasn’t just punishing them; He was protecting them. He didn’t let man mess up further by locking himself into a cursed eternity. God already had a plan for our eternal life, but it wasn’t going to be through that route.

Temporary remission

If you read the book of Leviticus, you will see how God instructed Moses on sacrifices for the forgiveness of sin. And if you pay close attention, you will notice something consistent—every sacrifice involved the shedding of blood. You might wonder why. Why not sing like Paul and Silas? If singing could break the prison gates, then it must be powerful. Why not cry before God? Why not pray and fast? Why was it always blood?

Because in the spirit realm, there is a law: without the shedding of blood, there is no forgiveness of sins (Hebrews 9:22). While we can pray for help and sing for breakthrough, the specific requirement for sin is blood offering. That is why animals were sacrificed in the Old Testament; but this system was temporary.

The high priest and tabernacle were only placeholders, pointing to something greater that was coming.

Please read Hebrews 9 entirely for deeper understanding. Leveticus too if you can.

The ultimate remission

Think of God as a Judge hearing a case against His own son for property damage. Because the Judge is perfectly Just, He doesn't bend the rules; He sentences His son to the full legal penalty of ₦20 million. Justice is served.

But then, the Father’s love moves. He steps down from the bench and pays the fine on His son's behalf.

Because the money is handed over, the court has no more claims against the boy. He is free. Not because the crime was ignored, but because the Father personally paid the price.

The debt is paid, the law is satisfied, and His son is free.

God’s plan was always for a final, perfect sacrifice. The repeated sacrifices of animals were not sufficient to permanently take away sin. If they were, we would still be offering sacrifices today.

In the Old Testament, only the high priest could enter the Holy of Holies to offer the sacrifices for the people. But when Jesus came, He became both the high priest and the sacrifice. He entered not an earthly tabernacle made by men, but the heavenly one (Hebrews 9:11). And unlike the priests who offered the blood of animals, Jesus offered His own blood. Once. For all.

But there is a great difference between Adam’s sin and God’s gracious gift. For the sin of this one man, Adam, brought death to many. But even greater is God’s wonderful grace and his gift of forgiveness to many through this other man, Jesus Christ. (Romans 5:15 NLT)

Christ became the perfect sacrifice and the mediator of a new covenant, giving us the eternal life God had promised from the beginning (Titus 1:2).

So what next?

Jesus told her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Anyone who believes in me will live, even after dying. Everyone who lives in me and believes in me will never ever die. Do you believe this, Martha?” (John 11:25-26 NLT).

Now that Jesus has paid the ultimate price on the cross of Calvary, the work for forgiveness is finished. He paid the price so you do not have to ever again. But the question remains: do you believe?

The only thing left for you to do is believe and confess. To believe that he died to restore you to God; to give you back the eternal life you lost through disobedience; and to fix what was broken in Eden.

Do you believe?

The message is very close at hand; it is on your lips and in your heart. If you openly declare that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. (Romans 10:8-10)

Before the call…

I’d like to put this out here.

In 2024, I was listening to a long teaching on salvation. My plan was to listen to the end and make the declaration during the altar call. Because the sermon was hours long, I broke it into parts to listen before bed.

The night I finally planned to finish it, I fell asleep. But shortly after, I woke up sensing a heavy negative presence in my room. I wasn’t spiritually sensitive back then, but I knew something was wrong. I was paralyzed and terrified, especially after hearing a strained, sharp, unnatural voice in the dead of the night.

It eventually left, but I was shaken. It wasn’t the first time I’d faced an “encounter” while trying to get closer to God. The first time it happened, I chickened out because of fear. But this time, I stayed. I went back, finished the message, said the prayer, and got saved.

Here’s the truth: Satan already has his final judgment; he is eternally damned. There is no redemption for him, so he wants as many people as possible to join him. He will prey on whatever he can to keep you away from Christ. In my case, it was fear. He knew that if he made the midnight hour scary enough, I’d stop seeking God. It wasn’t a coincidence; it was a tactic. For you, it might be guilt. The constant reminder of a sin from years ago, whispering that you’re too “filthy” to be washed.

But hear this today: You are not too far gone. Just come. Come afraid. Come unsure. Come filthy. Come with your guilt. Your sin isn’t too big, His blood covers all. Healthy people don’t need a doctor, but the sick run to one. Come home. You’ve been away for too long.

The Gospel

God loves you. He loves you completely and deeply, not withholding an atom of His love. He loved you enough to send His Son to come as a man and pay the ultimate price for your sins, because the repercussion for sin is death, and God did not want to see you, His beloved, die and be separated eternally from Him. He died, and resurrected; and through His resurrection, He gave to us His own life that can never end, so that we can be with Him once again. One spirit—forevermore.

If you believe, please say this prayer:

Dear God, I confess that I am a sinner in need of your mercy and grace. I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, that He died on the cross for me, and rose again on the third day for my victory. I believe in my heart and make confession with my mouth that Jesus is Lord. Thank you for saving me; and I receive this new life in Christ. Amen.

Father, thank you for the grace to share this message of the gospel. Thank you for every life that has come back to You through these words. Let you grace, mercy, and favour rest upon them, guide them, fill them, and let their lives bring glory to You, until we meet again in eternity. Amen.

And that’s it. Your sins are forgiven. Your sins are forgotten.

If you just got saved, or you need guidance, feel free to send a DM. I love you, but not nearly as much as God does. So bask in His love and enjoy the joy of salvation✨.

Welcome to the body of Christ ❤️.
