Bill your daddy today; he's not complaining

Mark 5:21-29 (NIV)

Published on Date • By Nicolletta Eneji • 5 min read

HELLO PEOPLEEE!!!

I was supposed to publish this newsletter on my birthday as my generous contribution to society. I learned this perspective on the 21st of February, and it felt like something worth sharing on a special day—my birthday, to be precise. Well… clearly, that didn’t happen. So here’s your slightly delayed gift. ❤️

In the month of February, I started studying the book of Mark. As you may already know, the first four books of the New Testament give different accounts of the same events in Jesus’ life (and if you didn’t know before now, you’re welcome).

When I got to Mark chapter 5, I realized I had read the story of “the woman with the issue of blood” months earlier in Luke. I’ll be honest—I wanted to skip the chapter because I was trying to move quickly through my Bible plan, but the Holy Spirit kept me there to teach me something new from a story I thought I already understood.

Let’s get into it.

21 When Jesus had again crossed over by boat to the other side of the lake, a large crowd gathered around him while he was by the lake. 22 Then one of the synagogue leaders, named Jairus, came, and when he saw Jesus, he fell at his feet. 23 He pleaded earnestly with him, “My little daughter is dying. Please come and put your hands on her so that she will be healed and live.” 24 So Jesus went with him.
A large crowd followed and pressed around him. 25 And a woman was there who had been subject to bleeding for twelve years. 26 She had suffered a great deal under the care of many doctors and had spent all she had, yet instead of getting better she grew worse. 27 When she heard about Jesus, she came up behind him in the crowd and touched his cloak, 28 because she thought, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be healed.” 29 Immediately her bleeding stopped and she felt in her body that she was freed from her suffering. (Mark 5:21 – 29 NIV)

Now, the usual takeaways from this story are faith and Jesus’ power, and rightly so. But that day, the Holy Spirit showed me something different:

The day you believe it is your turn… becomes your turn.

Let me explain.

Jairus had already secured Jesus’ attention. Jesus was literally on His way to his house. The moment, to everyone watching, belonged to Jairus—even though a crowd full of people, likely with their own problems, were following as well. But one person stood out: the woman with the issue of blood.

She didn’t stand out because of the severity of her issue, and no, I’m not just talking about her faith. She stood out because she decided that moment was her time, despite knowing that Jesus was currently on His way to heal someone else. She seized the moment, acknowledging she was in the presence of the One who could heal her, and she made that moment hers... even though the moment was “about Jairus” to everyone else.

Many times, when we attend meetings or services where God is clearly moving, we disqualify ourselves simply because what is being addressed doesn’t match our specific need.

You’re at a prayer conference, and prayers are going up for marriage—meanwhile, you’re trusting God for a job. So you withdraw.

You’re at a Sunday service, and the gifts of the Spirit are being distributed, but all you want is healing for that persistent pain—so you disengage. You fold your arms and stop participating because you think today’s service is not for you.

Whenever the move of the Spirit seems to be in a direction different from our individual problems, we start to think, “It’s not my turn,” or “This meeting isn’t for me; maybe next time.” We forget that God is all-sufficient and omnipresent. There is no schedule in heaven that says, "Today is for healing; tomorrow is for provision.”

God is not limited to one issue per meeting. NSPPD, Hallelujah Challenge, and Upper Room can all be happening at the same time, and God’s presence will be evident in all locations. Yet, you—who are in the same room where the Holy Spirit is moving—will suddenly decide it is not your turn because you need a job while the minister is announcing fruits of the womb.

The woman with the issue of blood understood that God isn’t limited. She saw that Jesus was headed somewhere else. She knew the attention wasn’t on her, but she also knew that “if He can do it, then He can do it for me—right now”.

We need to understand that as long as God is present, all you need to do is have faith and raise your concerns to Him. There is no “set day” for your issue. Your set day is the day you believe and lay it at His feet. Jesus never told anyone, “Today is not for you; come back tomorrow for your miracle,” so rest assured you won’t be the first.

The woman with the issue of blood clearly saw Jesus was on His way to Jairus’ house, but she knew He could heal her too. She didn’t care that the moment seemed to be for someone else; she knew He could handle her case simultaneously, so why not leverage the opportunity?

Tell God your needs and problems as they arise.

Pray about your rent even when the altar call is about the fruit of the womb.

Tell him about your exams even when the focus is on business breakthroughs.

Ask for a car to commute even when testimonies are about healing.

Your problem does not have to resemble someone else’s before God attends to it. If it worries you, it concerns your Father. And whenever you bring it to Him, He will answer.

So talk to Jesus today. Your set time is not someday; your set time is NOW.
